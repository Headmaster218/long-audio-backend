The replacement is an explicitly different **visible PID-namespace sampled-RSS protocol**, not an invisible relaxation of the earlier qualification. It captures the execution PID namespace and boot identity, verifies them on each sweep, and accounts for every visible numeric process within bounded enumeration and census limits. Unknown or unclassified roles are included. No comparator or role-based memory discount applies.

RSS reads are bracketed by kernel identity checks. Previously measured unreadable live identities retain conservative prior charges within the original fixed three-second freshness window; repeated observations do not grant a new deadline. A newly observed process that remains live but never yields RSS is refused rather than assigned zero. Partial readable RSS is retained even when a later read fails. Identity drift or reuse within a read is refused. Disappearance requires positive same-namespace absence and produces a durable exit-gap record; prior aggregate charge is retained for the raced sample.

The sampled charged-memory ceiling remains **6 GiB**. Workload CPU affinity and nice-19 constraints remain strict. Only the exact captured and verified infrastructure ancestors receive the documented CPU/nice exception, and their RSS is still charged. Role classification is diagnostic attribution, not resource-sample admission. Current attribution may therefore be incomplete and must not be substituted with historical observed roles.

This protocol measures sampled visible coverage with disclosed exit gaps. It is neither an atomic cgroup measurement nor a proof of historical peak memory. A process that exits before its first readable memory observation leaves an explicitly unmeasured gap. PID-namespace visibility also does not establish absence of processes elsewhere on the host.
